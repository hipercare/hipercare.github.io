The open-source core

Meet HiperHealth.

HiperHealth is the computational library at the heart of the plan. Its public Python library provides composable clinical workflow stages and a plugin system for installable “skills.”

Rather than one fixed pipeline, the architecture lets developers compose and extend the workflow. The ambition is to bring in the specialty capabilities needed for a particular care setting.

Where we are

An existing foundation.
A clear development agenda.

The public library and the planned Hipercare service are at different stages. Here is the distinction.

Documented in the library

A core to build on

  • Composable pipeline stages and installable skill plugins.
  • Persistent sessions that can resume as information arrives.
  • Checks for information required by a skill.
  • Built-in capabilities for diagnostic suggestions, report extraction, and de-identification.

Software functionality is not evidence of clinical effectiveness. See the library’s current documentation for scope and limitations.

Planned for Hipercare

A connected care experience

  • Longitudinal patient records connected to the care pipeline.
  • Medication interaction checks to support clinician review.
  • Pathways that account for protocols, coverage, and patient circumstances.
  • Future-risk insights to support prevention, subject to research and validation.

These are development intentions, not a list of live or clinically validated services.

Our open-source philosophy

Share the foundations.
Build with intention.

Hipercare is planned as a proprietary service. Its philosophy is to break down the technology into small, useful open-source projects, then orchestrate them into a coherent experience.

That distinction matters: an open component can be studied and improved independently, while the complete service has its own product responsibilities.

HiperHealth is one of those foundations. We welcome contributors who care about clear interfaces, reproducible workflows, documentation, and useful building blocks.
